**January 3, 2009.**
Satoshi Nakamoto engraves into the very first Bitcoin block:
*"Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks."*
A message to the world: the banking system is broken. There is another way.

**Paris, 1803.**
Napoleon Bonaparte creates the franc germinal. 1 franc = 290 milligrams of gold, written into law. This franc will hold for 110 years — an entire generation of French people knows exactly what their money is worth.

**Today.**
The franc germinal is dead. Gold no longer anchors anything. The euro, managed from Frankfurt by an institution no one elects, loses purchasing power every year. The French work more to buy less. No one asked for their opinion.

## The problem

Since the gold standard was abandoned, no currency protects purchasing power. Central banks create money. Inflation silently erodes your savings. What you put aside today will be worth less tomorrow. Always less.

This is not inevitable. It is a political choice that others made in your place.

---

## The solution

**ETL is the first digital currency indexed to the real purchasing power of the French people**, as measured every quarter by INSEE, the French national statistics institute.

> *1 ETL = what 1 euro of purchasing power was worth on the day of the first block.*

In 10 years, in 30 years, 1 ETL will still buy the same thing. This commitment is engraved in code — not in a law that a parliament can undo, not in a treaty that an institution can rewrite.

---

## What ETL is not

ETL is not a digital euro. It is not a share, a bond, or a financial product. It pays no interest. It is backed by no asset, no institution, no state. Its only value is the value the community freely recognizes in it.

ETL is a community currency. Its members are solely responsible for their exchanges.

## They have always done the same

This is not the first time a revolutionary idea has been captured by those it threatened.

**Napster — 1999**
Napster invents peer-to-peer music sharing. Free. No middleman. The record industry cries murder. Napster is killed in 2001. But the industry had understood something: people want digital music, accessible, instantly. It took the idea, monetized it, and turned it into a subscription. iTunes. Spotify. Deezer. Apple Music.

> *Napster wanted to free music. The industry killed Napster and sold its freedom back for €9.99 a month.*

**The Internet**
Designed as a decentralized network where every computer was equal. No center. No boss. Today the vast majority of global traffic flows through the servers of five American companies: Google, Amazon, Facebook, Apple, Microsoft.

> *The Internet wanted to decentralize information. Silicon Valley centralized it more than ever.*

**OpenAI — 2015**
Founded as a non-profit. Official mission: to develop artificial intelligence "for the benefit of all humanity". The name says it all: *Open* AI. Open. For everyone. In 2023, Microsoft invests 13 billion dollars. Today the most powerful models are closed, behind a paid subscription.

> *OpenAI wanted to free artificial intelligence for humanity. Today you pay €20 a month to Microsoft.*

**Bitcoin — 2009**
Bitcoin was created to be digital cash. You buy it, you hold it yourself, you spend it directly with someone else. No bank. No middleman. That is what Satoshi built.

Here is what happened next.

*Step 1 — The exchanges (2010).* People did not want to manage their own keys — too complicated, too risky. So companies offered to do it for them. Like a bank. You deposit your Bitcoin with them. They keep it. You trust them. Mt. Gox was born. First exchange, first betrayal. From that moment on: mandatory identity, funds held by a third party, regulation.

*Step 2 — Speculation (2013–2017).* Bitcoin became a speculative asset. People no longer bought it to pay — they bought it to sell higher. Wall Street smelled money.

*Step 3 — Institutional capture (2020–2024).* MicroStrategy, Tesla, then BlackRock, Fidelity. In January 2024 the US SEC approves Bitcoin ETFs. From now on you can buy "Bitcoin" from your banking app, like a share of Apple or TotalEnergies. You do not own Bitcoin. You own a piece of paper saying BlackRock owns Bitcoin for you.

The Bitcoin protocol has not changed by a single bit. It is still decentralized, still peer-to-peer. Satoshi did not lose technically. But humans rebuilt the banking system around it — out of convenience, out of fear, out of speculation.

> *Satoshi wanted to get rid of banks. Banks sell Bitcoin.*

The pattern is always the same:

```
1. A revolutionary idea is born — decentralized, free, for everyone
2. It disturbs the powerful
3. They cannot kill it — it is too good
4. So they buy it, lock it up, sell it
5. The revolution becomes a subscription
```

**ETL knows these stories. It will not repeat them.**

ETL is a currency for use — you use it, exchange it, spend it. It is not a speculative asset. It is not a financial product. It is not meant to be listed on a stock exchange or to become the plaything of investment funds.

Every member holds their own ETL. Their own keys. Their own freedom.

*The community collectively commits to resisting any attempt at institutional capture.*

## Immutable rules

*Engraved in the genesis block. No vote can change them. Ever.*

1. **1 ETL = 1 unit of the French national CPI on the day of the genesis**
2. **Issuance only through node validation** — every validated block requires proof of work (lightweight PoW, ~2 seconds on a standard CPU). Difficulty adjusts automatically to the number of active nodes. No proof of work = no ETL
3. **Zero initial allocation to the creator** — no pre-mine
4. **Every confirmed transaction is irreversible**
5. **Pseudonymity guaranteed** — the protocol requires no real identity
6. **No external authority** — no state, no central bank, no European Union, no organization of any kind. The only authority is the community of node operators
7. **1 member = 1 node maximum** — protection against manipulation
8. **All transactions are cryptographically signed** — verifiable by all, impossible to forge
9. **Every block contains the cryptographic fingerprint of the previous block** — altering a past block breaks the entire chain and is mathematically detectable by everyone
10. **ETL's source code is and will remain public, open and auditable by all**
11. **If INSEE does not publish the CPI for a quarter, the previous value is kept** until the nodes vote

## Governable rules
